Frequently asked

Where is my workout data stored?

Entirely on your device. RepLedger has no account, no server and no internet connection — nothing you log ever leaves your iPhone or iPad.

Because it lives on your device, your data is included in your normal iCloud or computer backups, and restoring a device from a backup brings it with you. Deleting the app deletes the data, and there is currently no in-app sync between devices.

Can I log in pounds instead of kilograms?

Yes — Settings → Weight Unit. Weights are always stored in kilograms and converted only for display, so switching units never changes anything you've already logged.

Does it support dark mode?

Settings → Appearance offers Light, Dark, or System. System follows your device, and is the default.

How do I fix a workout I logged wrong?

Open History, tap the workout, then tap Edit. It reopens in the same editor you logged it with, so you can correct reps and weights, or add and remove sets.

To delete a workout entirely, swipe left on it in the History list.

What does the PR badge mean?

It appears on a set that beats your best previous effort for that exercise, measured by estimated one-rep max — so it can trigger from lifting heavier or from more reps at the same weight.

How is estimated 1RM worked out?

With the Epley formula, applied to your best set in each session. It's an estimate for tracking trends over time, not a prediction of what you should attempt — treat it as a progress signal rather than a target.

Can I add an exercise that isn't in the list?

Yes. Tap Add Exercise, then New in the top corner, and give it a name, muscle group and equipment type. Your custom exercises sit alongside the built-in ones and are searchable the same way.

What are sections for?

They group a session into parts — Warmup, Main and Cooldown by default, or any name you like. Progress and personal records count every set you log, whichever section it sits in.

Privacy

RepLedger collects nothing. There's no analytics, no tracking and no third-party services, because the app has no networking at all.
